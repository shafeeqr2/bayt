# Design: Goal-based property search with on-demand conversion verdicts

## Problem Statement

A buyer states a goal in plain language, for example "a property I can turn into a legal triplex under $900k with $150k down".
Bayt judges each listing against that goal using the photos, the building layout, the lot, the zoning and the money, then returns a ranked list where every verdict carries evidence the buyer can check.
The deterministic strategy search (rent and cap-rate estimates, affordability, value-add flags, plain-language parser) exists on the backend as of 2026-10-16.
This design adds the part that reads the building.

## Demand Evidence

- The founder has talked to investors who screen conversion candidates by hand today.
  No investor was named during the session despite two requests.
  Treat this as a conversation, not demand, until the assignment below is done.
- No paid or committed user exists.
- Founder's own framing of the question: "do you think there is value?"

## Status Quo

Those investors judge "can this become a triplex" with a site visit plus a contractor's or designer's opinion.
That costs days and hundreds of dollars per candidate, and most candidates fail.
The real competitor is that workflow, not another app.

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge

- Target user: a small investor using their own money, doing one or two conversions a year, who pays contractors out of pocket to check candidates.
  Shape chosen by the founder; the actual person is still unnamed.
- Narrowest wedge: a ranked list for one goal only (legal triplex) over City of Toronto freehold listings, where each rejection shows the photo, the measurement or the by-law clause behind it, and each of the top candidates is one the investor would not have shortlisted by eye.
  Other goals keep the deterministic flags the strategy search already has and get no building read in this phase.

## Constraints

- No batch runs over the dataset.
  The founder's words: "dont run on the dataset, when i ask then run process on the listing".
  Analysis runs when a query asks for it, on the candidates the query narrows to, and is cached per listing, photo set and prompt version (the rubric does not depend on the goal).
- Model cost must stay small.
  The founder's words: "these models are way too expensive".
  One Haiku-class model fills a fixed rubric; there is no second, deeper model pass in this phase.
  Model name, photo cap, image size, the per-query vision call cap and a daily cost ceiling are environment settings, so a stronger model can be swapped in later without code changes.
- Data is scraped REALTOR.ca listings for feasibility only: "im doing this on a scraped dataset just to get a feel of if this is possible".
  A licensed feed is a later problem.
- GTA-wide is the goal; Toronto is where parcels, zoning, building outlines and lot analysis are complete today.
  Other municipalities join as rule tables and data, not as new code paths.

## Premises

1. First customer: the small investor above, who pays for site visits and contractor opinions to screen candidates.
2. In Toronto, multiplex permission is near-universal (727 of about 950 freehold listings in the database sit in multiplex-permitting zones), so legality is a commodity check there.
   Across the GTA, legality is a real but per-municipality data check: Mississauga allows fourplexes as-of-right, Markham's mayor vetoed them, Vaughan agreed to four units, the provincial baseline is three units.
   Everywhere, the discriminating question is whether the building and the economics work.
   Revised from "Toronto only" after founder pushback.
3. Reliable, explained rejections are the engineering core; the shortlist is the product promise.
   A wrong "yes" burns a site visit and trust fastest, and a shortlist is only worth paying for if the rejections behind it are right.
   Revised after the second opinion challenged the original "a confident yes is a bonus".
4. Verdicts are worthless without a ground-truth loop: site visits, permit outcomes and the investor's own findings feed back into the system.
   The accumulated verdict-versus-outcome dataset is the moat, not the model.
5. This phase is a feasibility exploration on scraped data.

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: Rubric screen, Toronto first

Deterministic gates over loaded data, then a Haiku-class vision rubric per surviving listing, a score with a reasons list, one page, feedback buttons.
Effort M (human about 2 weeks, Claude Code about 2 days). Risk low.
Reuses `app/strategy`, `app/lot`, `listing_city_data`, the parser and the `listing_investment` table pattern.
Chosen, with one change: it runs on demand, not as a batch (see Recommended Approach).

### Approach B: Goal engine with municipality rule tables

Goals as per-municipality rule sets in data, pluggable evidence sources, versioned verdicts, feedback and agreement metrics from day one, Sonnet escalation, GTA cities added by loading zoning and parcels.
Effort L. Risk medium.
Not chosen now: weeks of zoning data wrangling for five cities before the first verdict, and the stronger-model escalation it bundles is deferred for the same reason.
It is where Approach A grows once Toronto verdicts prove useful.

### Approach C: Per-address conversion report on demand

Paste an address, get a paid report; no ranking, search later.
Effort S/M. Risk medium.
Ruled out: it is not the goal-based search the founder asked for, and the shortlist moment never happens.

## Recommended Approach

Approach A, run lazily, for one goal.

1. A query enters through the existing parser (`backend/app/strategy/parse.py`) and gains a `goal`.
   This phase supports exactly one goal with a vision step: `triplex`.
   The enum reserves `fourplex`, `basement_suite`, `garden_suite` and `extra_bedroom` for later; a query naming one of those gets the existing deterministic value-add flags from the strategy search and a visible "no building read for this goal yet" note, never a vision call.
2. Deterministic gates run in SQL and Python over data already loaded.
   The table in step 5 is the complete gate inventory for the triplex goal: zone, ownership, frontage, interior size and budget are the blocking gates; height, coverage, existing suite and heritage are scoring-only.
   Lot depth, basement type, bedroom count and bath count are inputs to the rubric prompt and the score but are not gates and never set a verdict state.
   Each gate returns pass, fail or unknown with the evidence and its source (the parcel number, the by-law clause, the listing field).
   Gates cost nothing and run on every query.
3. Only listings that survive the gates get the vision step, and only when a query needs it.
   One call per listing to a Haiku-class model through OpenRouter (`OPENROUTER_API_KEY`, `OPENROUTER_MODEL`, photo cap and image size as settings).
   Input: up to eight downscaled photos and the gate results.
   Output: a fixed JSON rubric only: stair count and type visible, basement ceiling looks under 2.0 m (yes, no, unknown), separate side or rear entrance visible, basement windows egress-sized, interior gutted or finished, structural red flags, and the photo index behind each answer.
   No free-text verdict.
   The rubric describes the building and does not depend on the goal, so it is stored once in a new `listing_feasibility` table keyed by listing, photo-set hash and prompt version; goal-specific verdicts are computed from it in step 4.
   A listing is never sent to the model twice for the same photos and prompt.
4. Per-query vision budget.
   A query may trigger at most `VISION_CALLS_PER_QUERY` uncached model calls (default 25), spent on gate survivors in gate-score order.
   Survivors beyond the cap appear in the list as "not analyzed yet" with an "Analyze next 25" action; cached rubrics are always used regardless of the cap.
   The eight photos sent are the first four gallery photos plus up to four more whose listing caption or room order points at the basement, stairs or a side or rear exterior, in that priority; the photo-set hash is computed over that selected set.
   A `VISION_DAILY_COST_CEILING` setting (default $1) stops new calls for the day when reached and says so on the page.
5. Verdict states and scoring.
   Each listing gets one of three states for the goal: `pass`, `needs_check` or `reject`, plus a score and a reasons list.
   `reject` requires a failed gate or a rubric field that is a hard blocker with a photo behind it; the rejection leads with that single reason and its evidence.
   `needs_check` is any listing with no failure but at least one unknown blocking gate or rubric field; it shows which check is missing and why (for example "no basement photo in the listing").
   `pass` means every blocking gate and rubric field passed; the score orders passes by financials and non-blocking rubric fields.
   Ranking is passes by score, then needs_check by fewest unknowns, then rejects.
   An unknown never produces a rejection, so every rejection has evidence to show.
   Before any rubric exists, the vision budget is spent on survivors ordered by fewest unknown gates, ties broken by the financial score from `listing_investment`.

   Provisional classification of gates and rubric fields for the triplex goal.
   Every row is "to confirm" against the gate-list open question before it is encoded.

   | Check | Source | Blocking? | Fails when |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | Zone permits three or more units | `listing_city_data` zone, lot analysis `as_of_right_units` | blocking | zone outside the multiplex-permitting categories; unknown when the listing has no city match |
   | Freehold ownership | listing `ownership_type` | blocking | any condominium, co-operative or co-ownership type |
   | Lot frontage at or above the zone minimum | lot analysis `frontage_m`, zone minimum | blocking | frontage below the minimum; unknown without a lot analysis |
   | Interior size can hold three units | new `Listing.size_interior_m2` (from the raw `SizeInterior`, backfilled in the feasibility migration), then `sqft_max` | blocking | under 150 m² (about 1,600 sqft); unknown when the listing gives no size (approved D2 in the eng review) |
   | Within the buyer's budget and financing | query `max_price`, `income`, `down_payment` via the strategy search | blocking | price over the cap or the stress-tested payment over the GDS/TDS room |
   | Basement ceiling looks under 2.0 m | rubric | blocking | yes, with the photo it was judged from |
   | Structural red flags visible | rubric | blocking | yes, with the photo (foundation cracks, sagging floors or roof lines) |
   | Stairs: a single interior stair serving three storeys | rubric plus listing `stories` | scoring only, flagged "second exit likely needed" | never fails on its own |
   | Separate side or rear entrance visible | rubric, listing basement features | scoring only (positive) | never fails |
   | Basement windows egress-sized | rubric | scoring only (negative when no) | never fails; windows can be added |
   | Interior gutted or finished | rubric | scoring only | never fails |
   | Building height and storeys within the zone limit | building outline height, `height_limit_m` | scoring only | never fails; an existing building is already there |
   | Lot coverage under the 45% houseplex cap | lot analysis coverage | scoring only | never fails; informs how much can be added |
   | Existing suite or second kitchen | `listing_investment` suite flags | scoring only (positive) | never fails |
   | Heritage register listing | `listing_city_data` heritage status | scoring only (negative) | never fails |
6. Results stream to the page as they complete, deterministic results first, so a query over 50 candidates feels immediate while the rubric fills in.
7. Every listing carries three feedback buttons: visited and viable, visited and not viable, wrong reason.
   Feedback lands in a `feasibility_feedback` table with the verdict version it judged.
8. Geography is a config table: Toronto rules first, other GTA municipalities added as rows plus their zoning and parcel layers.

Trust rules: every reason names its source; "unknown" is a legal answer that never rejects; model output is never shown as prose, only as rubric fields next to the photo they came from.

Named follow-on, out of this phase: a stronger model pass on a single listing from its page, with its own extended rubric, once the Haiku rubric has been measured against the investor study.

## Open Questions

- Who are the two investors?
  Nothing in this design is validated until they exist by name.
- Which Ontario Building Code rules gate a triplex in practice?
  The sources point to egress and stair rules and side setbacks for five-plus units, but no authoritative checklist was found.
  An architect or the City's building division should confirm the gate list before it is encoded.
- How well do listing photos cover basements and side entrances?
  If most listings lack those photos, "unknown" will dominate and the rubric adds little.
- Does the per-municipality rule table stay small, or do site-specific by-laws and former-municipality by-laws make it unmanageable outside Toronto's 569-2013 area?
- What does a report cost to produce once Sonnet reads are included, and will anyone pay per report, per month, or per deal?

## Success Criteria

- The investor study, one task, run before either investor sees Bayt's output: each of two named investors sorts the same 30 Toronto freehold listings into "would visit", "would not visit" and "not sure" with a one-line reason each, then picks a top five from their "would visit" pile.
- Label agreement is scored per listing where both investors gave the same non-"not sure" label, with all four cells defined: Bayt `reject` on an agreed "would not visit" listing agrees; Bayt `pass` or `needs_check` on an agreed "would visit" listing agrees; Bayt `reject` on an agreed "would visit" listing misses; Bayt `pass` or `needs_check` on an agreed "would not visit" listing misses.
  Listings where the investors disagree with each other or either said "not sure" are excluded from the denominator and their count is reported next to the percentage.
  Target: agreement on at least 85% of scored listings, with at least 20 scored listings for the result to count; if fewer than 20 remain, the study grows until it reaches 20.
- At least one listing in Bayt's top five by score, absent from both investors' top fives, is confirmed viable by a contractor's opinion.
- Every rejection on the page shows a photo, a measurement or a clause the investor can check in under a minute.
- A query over 50 candidates returns deterministic results in under two seconds and completes the rubric for the first `VISION_CALLS_PER_QUERY` (25) uncached survivors in under a minute; the criterion assumes roughly half of 50 candidates survive the gates, so a typical query fits inside one budget.
- Model cost per analyzed listing stays under one cent at eight photos downscaled to about 1,000 px on `anthropic/claude-haiku-5.5` (OpenRouter list price $0.10 per million input tokens on 2026-10-16), so a full 25-call budget costs well under a quarter.
